# Scientific model notes

The HIV Atlas virion is a **structure-informed educational illustration**. It is designed to communicate spatial relationships and component identity without claiming atomic-resolution fidelity.

[Back to the README](../README.md)

## Design principle

The model prioritizes three questions:

1. What is the component?
2. Where is it located within a mature HIV-1 particle?
3. Which details are established biology, and which are illustrative simplifications?

The interface links each selectable structure to relevant research and describes important limitations beside the visualization.

## Structural decisions

| Feature | Model decision | Scientific rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall particle | Approximately spherical lipid envelope with subtle organic deformation | Mature HIV-1 has a host-derived membrane envelope; it is not an icosahedral virus with a rigid outer protein shell |
| Membrane | Opaque bilayer geometry with separate inner and outer lipid-head markers | The envelope is a lipid bilayer only a few nanometres thick |
| Env distribution | 14 irregularly distributed trimers | HIV-1 Env is sparse; reported counts are approximately 7–14 trimers per virion |
| Env composition | Three gp120 heads and three gp41 stalk/transmembrane components per trimer | Env is a trimer of gp120–gp41 heterodimers |
| Matrix | Thin, discontinuous p17/MA patches beneath the membrane | Matrix is membrane-associated and distinct from the internal capsid |
| Capsid | Closed, elongated, asymmetric fullerene cone with blunt ends | Mature HIV-1 CA forms a closed conical capsid, not an open droplet or decorative shell |
| CA lattice | Predominantly hexameric markers plus exactly 12 pentamers | CA hexamers form most of the lattice; 12 pentamers enable closed fullerene-cone topology |
| Genome | Two condensed, intertwined single-stranded RNA paths with NC markers | Two copies of positive-sense genomic RNA are packaged with nucleocapsid protein |
| Reverse transcriptase | Distinct molecular-style assembly enclosed by the capsid | RT is packaged with the genome and copies viral RNA into DNA after entry |
| Integrase | Distinct molecular-style assembly enclosed by the capsid | Integrase is carried within the capsid and inserts viral DNA into the host genome |
| Protease | Dimer inside the virion but outside the capsid | Protease cleaves Gag and Gag–Pol polyproteins during maturation |

These are explanatory model counts, not measurements from a single reconstructed virion. CA protein count, molecular packing, lipid positions, glycan structures, and internal organization are simplified.

## What the cutaway means

The intact particle is enclosed. The application swaps between separate intact and sectioned meshes to reveal the interior.

- The envelope cutaway is an educational removal of membrane geometry.
- The capsid teaching window appears only for appropriate internal-component views.
- The capsid-focused view retains a closed capsid.
- Whole-particle transparency is not used to fake dissection.

## Deliberate simplifications

- Scene units are arbitrary and the model is **not to scale**.
- Protein surfaces are stylized rather than atomistic.
- RNA paths communicate two condensed strands; they do not reconstruct the RNA secondary structure or a base-paired duplex.
- Glycan markers, lipids, matrix patches, and enzyme shapes are illustrative.
- The model does not simulate infection, molecular dynamics, or antibody binding.
- Relative internal placement should not be interpreted as a complete reconstruction of virion packing.

## Medical limitation

HIV Atlas provides general education only. It does not diagnose infection, assess an individual's risk, or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional. A possible exposure requiring PEP is time-sensitive: seek medical care immediately rather than waiting for website content or a test result.
